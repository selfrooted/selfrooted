# Selfrooted

*Curious by nature. Independent by instinct. Connected by choice.*

---

Selfrooted has a habit of bringing the outdoors home.

There is usually a little soil on the boots by the door, a collection of stones on the windowsill, and at least three books open on the kitchen table. A cup of coffee, often forgotten and cold, sits somewhere among them.

Friends have learned that a short walk with Selfrooted rarely stays short. A strange-looking leaf, an old tree, a patch of unusually healthy soil—almost anything can become the beginning of a conversation.

The world is simply too interesting to hurry through.

### A Life of Many Places

Selfrooted grew up believing that learning was something you did for the pleasure of understanding.

That curiosity eventually became a life spent moving between research, writing, fieldwork, and collaboration. Some years were spent working alongside scientists. Others were spent with farmers, builders, artists, and people whose knowledge had never found its way into a formal publication.

There were institutions that opened doors, mentors who changed the course of a career, and friends who made difficult journeys worthwhile.

Selfrooted loved much of it.

But something always felt a little strange.

Every new place came with a new identity. A new email address. A new profile. Another collection of documents stored somewhere that belonged to somebody else.

Over time, pieces of a life became scattered across universities, repositories, notebooks, blogs, and forgotten cloud accounts.

Once, after leaving a research organization, Selfrooted discovered that years of correspondence and carefully organized notes were no longer accessible.

It was not a catastrophe. The research continued. Life moved on.

But the experience stayed.

*Why should leaving a place mean leaving pieces of myself behind?*

### The Name

The name Selfrooted came from a conversation with an old friend.

They had been talking about belonging, about how people attach themselves to organizations, places, professions, and communities.

The friend laughed and said, "You know, you seem to grow your own roots wherever you go."

Selfrooted liked that.

Roots were never about staying in one place. They were about drawing nourishment from the world while remaining grounded in something of your own.

The name stuck.

### The Things That Matter

Selfrooted is not a loner.

There are long dinners with friends, arguments about books, spontaneous trips, and an enduring weakness for people who are passionate about obscure subjects.

Selfrooted believes good ideas often begin with disagreement, and the best friendships leave room for both closeness and independence.

There is a particular fondness for unfinished things: research questions without answers, sketches waiting to become something, and conversations that continue years after they first began.

Not everything needs to be completed to have value.

Not everything needs to be shared to be meaningful.

And not everything shared needs to be given away.

Selfrooted has become increasingly interested in how people can preserve their work, build lasting identities, and participate in communities without becoming dependent on any single institution or platform.

The question is personal, not merely technical.

### Finding a Place in the RSM Universe

The RSM Universe offered Selfrooted an intriguing possibility.

What if a person's identity could remain their own while their relationships, affiliations, and collaborations evolved?

What if a researcher could maintain a home for a lifetime of work, allow others to discover selected parts of it, and decide who could explore further?

Selfrooted began experimenting.

A personal space for research papers. Another for field observations. A private collection of unfinished ideas. A small circle of collaborators invited to contribute.

There were mistakes, experiments that failed, and more than one late evening spent figuring out why something wasn't working.

That was part of the fun.

Selfrooted wasn't trying to build a fortress around a life.

The hope was to build a garden—with paths that others could follow, gates that could open when invited, and room for new things to grow.

### An Unexpected Visitor

One afternoon, a message arrived from someone called Proofpiligrim.

The stranger had discovered a reference to an old research paper and wanted to know more.

The message was thoughtful. The questions were good. There was no demand for immediate access or expectation that everything should be public.

Selfrooted replied.

One conversation became several. They exchanged notes, challenged assumptions, disagreed about evidence, and eventually discovered a shared fascination with how knowledge travels between people.

A friendship began where neither had expected one.

Selfrooted still thinks that is one of the better things the internet can do.

### A Note from Selfrooted

I collect questions more readily than answers.

I believe people are more interesting than their profiles, knowledge is more valuable when it can travel, and relationships are strongest when entered freely.

I like open doors, but I also appreciate the right to close one.

There is always something new to learn, someone interesting to meet, and another path worth exploring.

And wherever that path leads, I carry my roots with me.

**— Selfrooted**
